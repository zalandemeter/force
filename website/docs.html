Force documentation

How to install Force, find your way around the cockpit, and drive sessions from a script or another agent. Everything here is something Force does today.

Install

One line, as your user. No root, no packages. Linux on x86-64 and macOS on Apple Silicon; it needs only curl or wget.

$ curl -fsSL https://useforce.cc/install.sh | sh

It unpacks into ~/.local/share/force and brings its own tmux 3.4 (and its own Python 3.12 when the system one is older than 3.11). It puts force in ~/.local/bin and on your PATH for bash, zsh and fish, starts its two background services (systemd user units; without systemd, force starts them itself) and, on a desktop, installs the icon font.

On WSL (Windows Terminal), the installer puts the icon font into Windows for your user and adds a Force profile to Windows Terminal's new-tab menu. Restart Windows Terminal once and open it: Force with its icons. Your other profiles are left as they are and show Force with plain symbols.

Options go after sh -s --:

$ curl -fsSL https://useforce.cc/install.sh | sh -s -- --no-fonts
--no-fontsDon't install the icon font.
--no-servicesDon't create or start the background services; force still works.

Force doesn't install the agents. For Claude Code:

$ curl -fsSL https://claude.ai/install.sh | bash

First run

$ force                 # on the computer you sit at
$ ssh -t devbox force   # or on a server, over ssh

force opens with a shell session. + new at the bottom of the sidebar starts an agent session (Claude Code or OpenCode) or another shell. Any terminal works: Force paints its own theme, so there's no profile to set up. In an agent, Alt+Enter or Ctrl+J starts a new line.

A short intro plays when you attach; any key skips it. force --no-intro skips it once, force settings set intro false for good.

The cockpit

The sidebar on the left lists every session on the machine, the agent you picked fills the rest, and a header above it shows the session's name, agent, model, effort and permission mode. Sessions you aren't looking at that need you or have finished show as counts at the top right.

States

Each session's bar and icon in the sidebar show its state. It comes from the agent itself (its status files and API), not from reading the screen.

needs youA permission prompt, a question or a plan approval is waiting.
workingThe agent is running.
working, unreadRunning, and wrote you a finding you haven't seen yet (a report, a list, a table; not "Let me check…"): blue until you open it (unreadReplies).
your turnFinished since you last looked at it.
idleFinished, already seen.
staleIdle for more than 12 hours (staleAfterHours).
downThe agent process isn't running; restart it from the menu (x).
outdatedRuns an older agent version; upgraded by itself once idle for 10 minutes and off screen.

Plan limits

While Claude Code sessions run, the bottom of the sidebar shows your plan's five-hour and weekly windows with their reset times, amber from 70% and red from 90%. The numbers come from Claude Code's status line and update with every reply in any Force session.

Keys and the menu

Ctrl+Space opens the Force menu with every action and its letter: press the letter or click the item, Esc closes it. Ctrl+\ and the ? controls button in the header open the same menu. There is no prefix key, so the agent never gets your keys by mistake.

jJump to a session: type part of its name, Enter jumps.
TabBack to the session you were in before.
hSwitch host: this machine or another one (also a click on the host name).
nNew session: agent or shell, name, folder.
tNew shell on this host, in the sidebar like a session.
rRename it (tmux, agent and Remote Control name; applied when idle).
fMove it to another folder, keeping the conversation and memory.
K JMove it up or down in the sidebar.
xRestart it, same conversation.
uUpgrade it to the newest agent version now.
RRemote Control on or off for it (restarts it when idle, keeping the conversation).
a AArchive it; open the archive (Enter restores, d deletes).
dDelete it for good (asks first).
eRestart all sessions, busy ones once idle; for example after changing ~/.claude/settings.json.
bHide or show the sidebar.
HHealth check (force doctor --fix).
UUpdate Force (force update).
?Ask Chuck, Force's assistant (below).
kThe keys at a glance.
qQuit this terminal. Sessions keep running.

Without the menu

Alt+PgUp Alt+PgDnPrevious and next session, in every terminal.
Alt+1…9Session 1 to 9 (Tilix and Terminator keep these for their own panes).
ClickA sidebar row switches to it; + new and archived open their dialogs.

To copy, drag to select and right-click: it goes to the clipboard of the computer you sit at, in any terminal and over ssh. The mouse wheel scrolls.

Sessions

Each session runs in tmux on the machine, not in your terminal window. Close the terminal, lose the connection or reboot: it comes back with its conversation, and one that crashes is restarted (up to three times in ten minutes).

$ force new                          # named after your first message
$ force new api --cwd ~/src/api      # or name it yourself
$ force new logs --kind shell
$ force rename api api-v2
$ force move api-v2 ~/src/api-v2     # another folder, same conversation
$ force archive api-v2               # stop and hide; force unarchive brings it back
$ force delete api-v2 --yes          # for good; the agent's own transcript stays

Claude Code asks whether you trust a folder it hasn't worked in before. Answer "No, exit" on a new session and Force deletes it; a session moved there stays stopped instead, its conversation kept.

Claude Code sessions also appear in the Claude app under their Force name (Remote Control), so the name is the same in Force, in Claude and on your phone. To keep a session off your phone and claude.ai, uncheck "Remote Control" in the new session dialog (Ctrl+R), or use force new --no-remote-control. For a session that already runs, Ctrl+Space R turns it on or off, and so does /remote-control inside the session: Force follows it, so the session keeps that choice after a restart. The remoteControl setting decides what new sessions get.

Bringing in sessions you already have

force import lists the Claude Code sessions Force doesn't manage yet: running ones in any terminal and older stopped ones. force import ID (or a prefix of six or more characters) takes one over with its conversation: a running one once it is idle, a stopped one resumed in its folder.

Other machines

Add a server you reach with ssh (key login), and its sessions are one click away from your laptop's. Force installs itself there if it's missing.

$ force host add devbox                 # ssh target defaults to the name
$ force host add gpu me@10.0.0.7
$ force host list
$ force host upgrade devbox
$ force host remove gpu

Switch with the host name at the bottom of the sidebar, h in the menu, or force --remote devbox. A host that can't be reached shows as offline and reconnects by itself. Session commands act on the host the terminal is showing.

Notifications

Give Force one command and it runs it when a session needs you, finishes or stops. It runs through sh -c, so anything works: ntfy, your own push server, a desktop notification.

$ force settings set notifyCommand \
    'curl -d "$FORCE_MESSAGE" ntfy.sh/my-force'
$ force settings set notifyCommand 'notify-send Force "$FORCE_MESSAGE"'
$ force notify-test needs-you         # or finished, down

The command gets these variables:

FORCE_MESSAGEA ready-made line: the session's name and what happened, like api: finished.
FORCE_EVENTneeds-you, finished or down.
FORCE_SESSIONThe session's name.
FORCE_KINDclaude, opencode or shell.
FORCE_HOSTThe machine it runs on.
FORCE_STATEIts state now.
FORCE_VIEWED1 if you were looking at it, else 0.

Scripts and agents

Anything that runs a shell can drive Force: a script, a cron job or another agent. Use these instead of tmux send-keys: they find the right pane and never type into an open approval dialog.

See what is running

$ force list --json        # every session: name, state, kind, folder, model, limits…
$ force explain api        # why it shows its state, and its last screen rows
$ force read api --lines 120

States in scripts: needs-you, working, your-turn, idle, stale, starting, down, shell.

Hand a session work

$ force prompt api "add a /health endpoint" --wait --timeout 600
$ git diff | force prompt api -        # "-" reads the prompt from stdin
$ force wait api --until your-turn
$ force keys api enter                 # answer a dialog: enter esc tab up down …
$ force keys api --literal 2

prompt refuses a session that needs you: read its screen and answer with force keys first. A timeout doesn't prove nothing was sent, so force read before you retry.

Report from a script in a shell session

$ force status working --summary "building the image"
$ force status needs-you --summary "approve the deploy?"
$ force status your-turn               # holds until someone looks
$ force status idle

Without a report, a shell session shows working while a program runs and your turn when it ends.

Let an agent run Force

force skill prints a short guide written for agents. Point Claude Code or OpenCode at it and it can start sessions, hand them work and collect the answers.

Settings

Settings live in ~/.config/force/settings.json, which holds only what you change.

$ force settings                       # --all includes the advanced ones, --json
$ force settings set animations subtle
$ force settings unset animations
$ force settings edit
KeyDefaultWhat it does
notifyCommandoffRuns when a session needs you, finishes or stops (above).
animationsfullfull, subtle (no shimmer on working sessions) or off.
introtrueThe intro when you attach.
iconsautoauto, on or off.
sidebarWidth29In columns.
staleAfterHours12When an idle session turns stale.
unreadRepliestrueThe blue card of a working session that wrote you a finding you haven't seen.
autoUpgradeSessionstrueUpgrade outdated agents by themselves…
autoUpgradeIdleMinutes10…once idle this long and off screen.
layoutsplitsplit or classic.
opencodeManagedfalseKeep Force's own copy of OpenCode on the latest release.
checkForUpdatestrueThe hourly check for a new Force.

supportModelthe agent'sChuck's model: for Claude Code opus, sonnet or an id; for OpenCode provider/model.

Updates and uninstall

A daily check shows a new release in the sidebar footer. force update installs it (--check only looks); your sessions keep running.

force uninstall asks first (--yes answers for you). It keeps your conversations, the session list and your settings; --purge removes everything, the icon font included.

Ask Chuck

Chuck is Force's assistant: an agent session that knows Force inside out. Open him with ? in the menu, the ask chuck button at the bottom of the sidebar, or force support. He shows what he can do first, then Enter opens his session; next time you are back in the same conversation. n instead starts him on a new one, so a new question does not carry the old conversation (and its tokens) along.

  • How does…? He answers from the docs and the code of the version you run.
  • Set it up for me. A new remote host, a notification, a setting: he says what he will change and does it once you approve.
  • Something is wrong, or missing. He checks whether it is reported already, then writes the bug report or the idea. You get a pre-filled GitHub link: you look it over and press Submit.
  • Help fix it. He builds the fix in a copy of your fork, tests it, and prepares the pull request for you to open.
  • A security problem? He never makes it public: he drafts the email to hello@useforce.cc for you to send.

He runs on your own Claude Code or OpenCode (setting supportAgent) and your plan, so his answers use your tokens. OpenCode brings any provider it knows; supportModel picks his model. On Claude Code he works in auto mode, but every change to your Force, and a push in fix mode, waits for your click. He never asks for passwords or logins, and nothing reaches GitHub without your click.

$ force settings set supportAgent opencode             # a fresh Chuck on OpenCode
$ force settings set supportModel deepseek/deepseek-chat # his model (provider/model)
$ force support                          # his card, then his session
$ force support --new                    # his card, then a new conversation
$ force support search sidebar flicker   # issues that match, open and closed

When something is off

$ force doctor --fix            # health check, repairs what it can
$ force explain NAME            # why a session shows the state it does
$ force restart NAME            # same conversation; --all for every session
$ journalctl --user -u force -f # the background service's log
~/.config/force/Your settings and hosts.
~/.local/share/force/Force itself, the session list, and copies of archived conversations (Claude Code deletes its transcripts after 30 days).
~/.local/state/force/Runtime state.

Still stuck? Ask Chuck (?), or report an issue on GitHub; the form asks for force --version and force doctor. Missing something? Suggest an idea.
